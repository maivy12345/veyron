Why ‘we’ll firm it up on site’ is a temporary-works red flag

Veyron Infrastructure EngineeringTechnical insight

There is a phrase that should make any temporary-works coordinator uneasy: “we’ll firm it up on site.” It usually means a design that is not finished being treated as if it were, with the gaps to be closed by judgement in the moment — which is exactly when judgement is worst.

Temporary works are loaded once and fail fast. The time to resolve a question about a prop spacing or a platform load is at the desk, with calculations and a check, not at the trench edge with a deadline and a digger waiting.

Proper control closes the loop before loading: design, independent check at the right category, inspection, permit. Each step exists because the previous one is not, on its own, enough.

When we hear ‘firm it up on site,’ we treat it as an unfinished design — and finish it, properly, before anyone relies on it.
