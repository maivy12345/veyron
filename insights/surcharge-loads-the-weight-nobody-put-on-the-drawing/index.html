Surcharge loads: the weight nobody put on the drawing

Veyron Infrastructure EngineeringTechnical insight

Temporary-works failures often involve a load that was real on site but absent from the design — the surcharge. Spoil heaped beside a trench, plant tracking near an edge, materials stacked on a working platform: each adds load the original design may never have considered.

Surcharge is dangerous precisely because it is informal. It is not a designed load delivered through a designed path; it is whatever ends up near the works, often placed by people who never saw the calculation.

Sound temporary-works design assumes realistic surcharge from the start and states the limits clearly — how close plant may track, how high spoil may be heaped — so the site knows the rules the design depends on.

We design for realistic construction surcharge and make the limits explicit, so the load that ends up on site is the load the design allowed for.
