When the right answer is to say no to a method statement

Veyron Infrastructure EngineeringTechnical insight

A method statement’s job is to describe a safe, executable way to do the work. Most are approved with comments; some should not be approved at all, and the discipline to say so is part of doing the job properly.

A method statement that omits the temporary works it depends on, ignores the surcharge the plant will impose, or sequences a pour before the support is verified is not a document to be improved with a comment — it is a plan that should not proceed until it is right.

The pressure to approve is real: the programme wants to move, and ‘no’ is unpopular. But an unsafe method statement waved through with optimistic comments is exactly how incidents are authorised on paper.

We review method statements honestly and are willing to withhold support when the method is not yet safe, because the alternative is signing off on a plan we know is not ready.
