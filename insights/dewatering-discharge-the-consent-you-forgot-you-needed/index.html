Dewatering discharge: the consent you forgot you needed

Veyron Infrastructure EngineeringTechnical insight

Dewatering design usually focuses on keeping the excavation dry. But the water has to go somewhere, and where it goes is often the part that gets a project into trouble — with the works and with the regulator.

Discharging groundwater is rarely a free act. It may need consent, treatment to remove silt or contaminants, and control of where and how fast it is released, so it does not erode, flood or pollute. A dewatering plan that pumps without a discharge plan is half a design.

The failures are predictable: a site stopped because discharge was unconsented, or an adjacent area damaged by uncontrolled release. Both are avoidable by treating discharge as part of the design, not an afterthought.

We design groundwater control as a complete system — extraction, monitoring and responsible discharge — so the water leaves the excavation safely and lawfully, not just quickly.
