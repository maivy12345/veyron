Working platforms: the ground beneath the crane nobody designed

Veyron Infrastructure EngineeringTechnical insight

A crane is only as stable as the platform it stands on, yet the working platform is one of the most under-designed elements on many sites. The machine is engineered to tolerances; the ground it tracks and lifts from is often just ‘made up’ and hoped to hold.

Working-platform failure is sudden and severe: an outrigger punches through, a tracked machine tips. The cause is almost always a platform whose bearing capacity was never matched to the loads the plant actually imposes.

Proper design treats the platform as a structure: assess the ground, the plant loads, and the required bearing, and specify the platform to suit — granular fill, geogrid, or a designed mat. It is engineering, not earthmoving.

We design working platforms to the real plant loads and ground conditions, so the machines that build the project stand on something engineered, not assumed.
