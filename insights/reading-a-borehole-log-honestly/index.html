Reading a borehole log honestly

Veyron Infrastructure EngineeringTechnical insight

A borehole log is one of the most consequential — and most over-trusted — documents in geotechnical engineering. It is a record of what was found at one point, to one depth, on one day. The temptation is to read it as the truth about the whole site; the discipline is to read it as a sample.

Honest interpretation asks what the log does not say as much as what it does: how the ground might vary between boreholes, how representative the samples are, where the investigation was thin. The conditions that cause trouble usually live in the gaps the boreholes did not reach.

This is why we reason conservatively where data is limited and recommend further investigation where the risk justifies it, rather than extrapolating a single log across a whole site as if it were certainty.

We read ground investigation for its limits as well as its findings, so the design respects the ground that was not sampled, not just the ground that was.
